## 1. Consensus themes, ranked by credibility × frequency

### Tier 1 — strongly corroborated across independent sources

**1a. Slow (daily-bar, days-to-weeks) systematic futures trading is the best-documented retail-viable approach — and the only one with a public, auditable live record.**
Rob Carver (ex-AHL portfolio manager, trades his own ~£500k futures account, posts on EliteTrader as *globalarbtrader*) publishes annual real-money reviews since 2014: [year-11 update](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2025/04/annual-performance-update-returneth.html) (worst futures year, −16.3%, vs ~−18% benchmark — he publishes losses too, which is the credibility marker) and [year-12](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2026/04/annual-performance-update-year-12.html) (+6.4% total, futures-driven). A 2026 EliteTrader meta-analysis, ["Hot or Not: EliteTrader's Journals, measured"](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/hot-or-not-elitetraders-journals-measured.390732/), rebuilt 42 ET journals trade-by-trade against market data: **nobody scored 5/5 on verifiability, most journals couldn't be graded at all, and the one author worth following was globalarbtrader** — "real money, automated, since 2014, with the blotters and drawdowns to prove it." Out of an entire forum's public journals, the sole reconstructible outperformer runs slow, diversified futures trend/carry.

**1b. The consensus "retail edge" is structural, not speed: capacity-constrained niches, risk premia, and unglamorous trades institutions won't touch.**
[QuantStart](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Can-Algorithmic-Traders-Still-Succeed-at-the-Retail-Level/): retail wins via "greater freedom to play in smaller markets… can generate significant returns in these spaces" and staying uncorrelated to crowded institutional trades — explicitly *not* via speed. Kris Longmore / [Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com/no-magic-formulas-how-i-actually-decide-what-to-trade/) (unusually honest that "everything decays and eventually disappears in this game," [index of strategies](https://robotwealth.com/index-of-strategies/)) names the durable families: **risk-premia harvesting in liquid ETFs, VIX basis / vol-risk-premium timing, equity pairs, crypto carry (perp funding vs spot), FX intraday seasonality, leveraged-ETF tracking-error trades** — edges that "look unattractive — noisy, awkward, too small for institutions — which is exactly why they persist." His crypto-carry work ([The Art and Science of Trading Carry](https://robotwealth.com/the-art-and-science-of-trading-carry/)) documents sticky perp funding premia as a genuine, retail-accessible return source, with the caveat that returns correlate with the premium and blow up in tails (delta-neutral implementation and exchange risk are the real work). Independent corroboration of the funding-arb math appears in [a peer-reviewed CEX/DEX study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2096720925000818) and practitioner guides ([ArbitrageScanner](https://arbitragescanner.io/blog/crypto-funding-rate-arbitrage-strategy-guide)).

**1c. Swing mean-reversion in equities (2–5 day holds) survives realistic costs — but the edge is shrinking.**
Cesar Alvarez (ex-head of research at Connors Research, publishes rules + cost assumptions): [a simple Russell-1000 pullback system](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/simple-ideas-for-a-mean-reversion-strategy-with-good-results/) — 100d MA up-filter, 3 lower lows, limit entry at 0.5×ATR below, exit first up close — **22.3% CAGR, 21% maxDD, 2004–2014, with $0.01/share commission+slippage included**, avg hold ~2.6 days. He is candid that ["the edges seem to get smaller"](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/category/stocks/) and entry-timing/limit-order mechanics decide whether it survives costs ([entry-timing study](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/mean-reversion-entry-at-open-vs-intraday-pullback-vs-confirmation/)). Ernie Chan's long-standing position matches: mean reversion in liquid names trading "at most twice a day" clears retail transaction costs; faster than that mostly doesn't ([epchan blog](http://epchan.blogspot.com/2008/12/enduring-profitability-of-mean.html)), and pairs are a crowded-trade risk.

### Tier 2 — credible but conditional

**2a. Intraday works for retail only via universe selection, not signal speed.** The best-argued recent thread found, ET's ["Realistic Sharpe Ratios in 2026: HFT vs. Retail Algos"](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/realistic-sharpe-ratios-in-2026-hft-vs-retail-algos-deep-dive.388680/): Sharpe >5 = HFT market-making via order-book imbalance, "impossible without co-located FPGAs… the alpha is purely technological speed"; realistic sophisticated-retail ceiling ≈ Sharpe 2–3, and the cited path is **trading only the ~20 "in-play" stocks/day (massive RVOL + catalyst: earnings, FDA)** — the alpha "is entirely in Universe Selection." Consistent with this platform's discovery-screener direction and inconsistent with fixed-universe 1m bar trading.

**2b. Vol-premium selling: real premium, mostly beta.** The most rigorous free retail-side backtests ([Spintwig, SPY wheel 45-DTE, 2,200+ trades, costs on](https://spintwig.com/spy-wheel-45-dte-options-backtest/)) find **~94% of wheel-strategy return is just the long-SPY exposure**; short options add little after costs. r/thetagang enthusiasm is mostly repackaged beta — defined-risk premium selling is viable but is a risk premium, not alpha.

### Tier 3 — the overnight effect: a cautionary special case (directly relevant to `overnight_reversal`)

The close-to-open drift was the forum darling of 2020–2023. It then failed *in implementation and in the data*: the NightShares ETFs (NSPY/NIWM) built on it launched June 2022 and **liquidated 14 months later**, NSPY −3% vs +15% for the S&P over its final year ([etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/2-nightshares-etfs-close-after-struggling-gain-traction), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-07-19/-night-effect-funds-to-shut-down-with-overnight-returns-elusive)); practitioners noted the anomaly "is impossible to easily exploit because of trading costs." The NY Fed's July 2026 ["The Disappearing Overnight Drift"](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/) quantifies it: the 2:00–3:00am ET futures drift worth ~3.7%/yr 1998–2020 **averaged ~zero 2021–2025**. Lesson: a published, capacity-thin microstructure anomaly decayed within a couple of years of popularization — the archetype of what forum consensus recommends *at the moment it stops working*.

## 2. The 1–2 minute horizon question (dedicated)

Every credible source found says the same thing, from different angles: **minutes-scale bar trading in liquid US equities/index futures is structurally cost-negative for retail**, matching the project's own falsification.

- **Arithmetic from futures practitioners:** ES round-trip commission ~$4/contract consumes **32% of a 2-tick scalp** (10.7% of 3 ticks), which is "why professional scalpers target 3–4 ticks minimum" ([futures scalping cost guides](https://www.tradealgo.com/trading-guides/futures/es-futures-scalping-guide)); measured live slippage across ~1,000 retail futures trades averages **0.8–1.2 ticks/trade, negative-skewed, 3–5 ticks on news** ([PickMyTrade broker slippage data](https://blog.pickmytrade.io/real-slippage-data-1000-trades-futures-brokers-2026/), unattributed methodology — indicative only). One tick of adverse slippage per side on MES ($1.25) plus commission is larger than any plausible 1m-bar signal.
- **The strongest recent empirical post:** ET's ["I backtested 37 'famous' strategies with fees ON"](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/i-backtested-37-famous-strategies-with-fees-on-most-lose-mechanically-after-costs-data-one-that-didnt.390197/) — 106 backtests, ~99k trades: "a big chunk of the famous setups are breakeven-to-positive gross and go negative once realistic costs are subtracted." The *single* survivor was a 5m NQ trend-pullback scalp (PF 1.25, 62.9% WR, +17.5%/80 days) — **with commissions only, no slippage, and the OP conceded "on a 5m NQ scalp with a 0.75R target, slippage is exactly where this strategy dies."** Even the best case in a 37-strategy sweep fails once slippage is honest.
- **Structural arguments:** the ET Sharpe thread: "stop trying to predict tick-by-tick direction of ES unless you are an HFT"; sub-5-minute bars are "essentially trading market noise" and pit you against co-located firms ([Why retail algo-traders fail](https://billiondollaralgorithms.com/blog/why-retail-algo-traders-fail), which prescribes 30m+ bars); adverse selection on resting limit orders intensifies as horizon shrinks ([adverse selection of meta-orders](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.27334)). QuantConnect's own forum treats minute-resolution intraday equity results as suspect by default because LEAN's **default slippage model is zero** ([thread 7756](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/7756/intraday-equity-trading-fees-slippage-etc/), [3048](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/3048/on-the-importance-of-slippage-please-read-this/)) — a systematic reason the community's "profitable" minute-scale backtests are illusory.
- **Crypto variant:** spot taker fees ~10–15bp and perp taker ~4–5bp ([fee guides](https://www.bitdegree.org/crypto/tutorials/binance-fees)) exceed any plausible 1m alpha for a non-VIP retail account; the retail-viable short-horizon crypto trades are maker-rebate market-making (capacity-tiny, ops-heavy) and funding capture — not directional minute bars.
- **The only partial dissents:** (a) in-play/catalyst intraday equity trading (universe selection), and (b) crypto maker-side execution — both of which relocate the edge away from the bar-signal itself.

**Bottom line:** the practitioner record independently reproduces the project's finding. Nobody credible reports sustained net-positive retail results from generic 1–2m bar signals on liquid equities or MES/MNQ/ES/NQ. The corroborated migration path: same platform, slower signals (hours–days), or event/universe-conditioned intraday.

## 3. Failure modes and post-mortems ("why your backtest lies")

1. **Backtest metrics don't predict live performance — at scale.** Quantopian's own study of 888k backtests ("All That Glitters Is Not Gold") found backtest Sharpe had ~no predictive value out-of-sample; the crowdsourced-alpha model died with the company in 2020 ([QuantRocket's 3 takeaways](https://www.quantrocket.com/blog/quantopian-shutting-down/), [Michael Harris](https://mikeharrisny.medium.com/quantopian-shutdown-offers-some-important-lessons-f8608535b7db), [What Works in Trading retrospective](https://whatworksintrading.substack.com/p/the-rise-and-fall-of-quantopian-lessons)). Alumni lesson: overfitting + ignored slippage/liquidity were rampant even among 200k quant-literate users, and "research requires theory supporting the data."
2. **Zero-cost defaults in retail platforms.** LEAN's default slippage = 0; NinjaTrader/TradingView strategy scripts likewise. Most community "profitable" systems die on this alone.
3. **Anomaly decay after publication/productization.** Overnight drift (NY Fed, NightShares); Alvarez on shrinking mean-reversion edges; Robot Wealth's blunt "everything decays."
4. **Durability base rates.** ET folklore in [thread 381244](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/do-automated-strategies-that-can-generate-consistent-profits-really-exist-worth.381244/): of ~500k automated strategies, ~1,000 survive a year, ~100 five years, ~5 ten years (unverifiable numerology, but the *shape* — intraday tick strategies living ~6 months — is repeated by many long-tenure posters).
5. **Unfalsifiable journals.** The ET journal audit: most public track-record claims "never write down enough to be wrong." Default prior for any forum claim without blotters: LARP.
6. **Professional-adjacent consensus (Blind):** ["Is solo algotrading a waste of time"](https://www.teamblind.com/post/is-solo-algotrading-a-waste-of-time-sb54cz7f) — Two Sigma poster: yes for individuals; "the dumb simple shit has all been arbed away long ago"; the one claimed-profitable poster cited thousands of hours, no numbers.

## 5. Brokers, data, venues, real costs reported

- **Futures (MES/MNQ/ES/NQ):** NinjaTrader/Tradovate/AMP+Rithmic/IBKR. All-in RT ≈ $1–1.3 (micros) to ~$4 (ES) incl. exchange fees; live slippage 0.8–1.2 ticks avg (Rithmic lowest ~0.7, Tradovate ~1.2) ([PickMyTrade data](https://blog.pickmytrade.io/real-slippage-data-1000-trades-futures-brokers-2026/)). CME market-data fees and micro-contract commission-to-tick ratios are the recurring complaints — micros halve risk but roughly double relative cost drag.
- **Equities:** IBKR (API, ~$0.005/sh or tiered) and Alpaca (free but PFOF; QC forum treats its zero-cost fills skeptically). Practitioner cost benchmark for swing backtests: $0.01/share all-in (Alvarez).
- **Options:** ~$0.65/contract + wide spreads; Spintwig models mid-fills and still finds thin net premium.
- **Crypto:** Binance/Bybit/OKX; base taker 4–15bp is the binding constraint; maker rebates (to −1.5bp on Bybit programs) are what make short-horizon work possible at all; funding arb needs ≥$2–3k just to beat fee drag.

## 6. Credible-corroborated vs claimed-but-unverifiable

**Credible, corroborated:** slow futures trend/carry (Carver — the single best-documented retail record); swing equity mean-reversion with costs on (Alvarez, Chan), edge decaying; risk-premia/carry framing incl. crypto funding capture (Robot Wealth + academic corroboration); vol selling as beta (Spintwig); minutes-scale directional trading being cost-negative for retail (converging: ET 37-strategies test, ET Sharpe thread, cost arithmetic, QC slippage threads, our own result); backtest→live non-transfer at population scale (Quantopian).

**Claimed but unverifiable (discount):** the "500k strategies, 5 survive a decade" figures; nearly all forum/journal P&L claims (ET audit: ~0/42 fully verifiable); Blind/Reddit "I quit my job off my algo" anecdotes; anything sold (courses, signal Substacks, TradingView invite-only scripts, AI-bot promos); the surviving 5m NQ scalp from the 37-strategy sweep (no slippage modeled, by the author's own admission).

**Coverage gaps:** r/algotrading/r/quant threads could not be directly accessed from the research environment; Nuclear Phynance is defunct (archive.org also blocked); Wilmott's relevant threads didn't surface via search. These are the places a follow-up with browser access should hit first.
