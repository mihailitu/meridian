# Appendix D — Medium-horizon systematic strategies (2 days – 3 months)

> Deep-research thread output, 2026-08-29. Part of `docs/research-survey-2026-08.md`.
> "Net" numbers flagged with their cost assumptions. Verdicts distinguish
> **risk-premium harvesting** (real, persistent, compensation for risk — not alpha)
> from **claimed alpha** (decays, needs rebuttal-checking).

## 1. TSMOM/trend — refinements only

**Vol-targeting is doing more work than the signal.** [Kim, Tse & Wald (2016, J. Empirical Finance)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2786955): MOP-style TSMOM alpha drops from ~1.08%/mo vol-scaled to 0.39%/mo unscaled ([summary](https://quantpedia.com/deconstructing-the-time-series-momentum-strategy/)); unscaled TSMOM ≈ buy-and-hold. Implication for T1: implement vol-targeting as a first-class component (it's most of the historical Sharpe), and benchmark T1 against a *vol-targeted long-only* portfolio, not raw buy-and-hold — otherwise risk-parity beta gets attributed to the trend signal.

**Statistical fragility.** [Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020, JFE) "Time-Series Momentum: Is It There?"](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3165284): little asset-by-asset predictability in- or out-of-sample; pooled t-stats fail bootstrap critical values — TSMOM profits largely reflect unconditional mean returns. Zakamulin & Giner (2022) reach related robustness concerns ([Alpha Architect discussion](https://alphaarchitect.com/are-trend-following-and-time-series-momentum-research-results-robust/)). The strongest academic rebuttal to MOP; it doesn't kill trend, but caps the honest prior at "modest premium," not in-sample Sharpe ~1.

**Speed blend.** [Man Group "The Need for Speed" (2023)](https://www.man.com/insights/need-for-speed-trend-following): after costs, *faster* speeds have materially lower long-run Sharpe but better skew/crisis convexity; slower speeds have higher net SR but worse crash response. Prescription: blend of speeds, weighted toward medium/slow ([Alpha Architect on fast/slow](https://alphaarchitect.com/trend-following-timing-fast-and-slow-trends/)). Baltas–Kosowski-style refinements (better vol estimators, trade-smoothing) cut turnover ~one-third with no significant performance loss ([paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255856103_Demystifying_Time-Series_Momentum_Strategies_Volatility_Estimators_Trading_Rules_and_Pairwise_Correlations)) — directly relevant to a small account's cost budget.

**Short-term trend is specifically dead.** ["Is Trend Still Your Friend? A Microstructural Account of the Demise of Short-Term Trend-Following" (arXiv 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01550): erosion of sub-weekly/fast trend from the early 2010s (crowding + microstructure), while weeks-to-months horizons remain comparatively intact. For the 2-day–3-month band: stay ≥ ~3-week lookbacks.

**Dead-or-alive, 2022–2026.** 2022 was the showcase (SG Trend +27%); March 2023 SVB shock caused a sudden drawdown; 2024 saw positive correlation to risk assets and repeated whipsaws — *longer-term* signals outperformed shorter ([AlphaSimplex, Themes of 2024](https://www.alphasimplex.com/assets/files/themes-of-2024---kaminski-and-zhao.pdf)) — and in the 2025 equity correction CTAs conspicuously failed to respond ([Capstone](https://www.capstoneco.com/insights/from-hero-to-villain-is-there-a-problem-with-trend-following/); [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/is-this-time-different); counterpoint: [Cazadores](https://www.cazadoresinvestments.com/2025/03/27/trend-following-is-not-dead-how-ctas-are-outpacing-traditional-hedge-funds/)). **Crisis-alpha refinement:** trend hedges *slow* bear markets (2008, 2022) and fails *fast* shocks (Feb 2018, Mar 2020 initially, Mar 2023, Aug 2024) — don't sell T1 to yourself as a crash hedge.

**Verdict:** viable retail thread on micro futures; expect net Sharpe 0.3–0.5, mostly risk premium + vol-targeting, not alpha.

## 2. Volatility risk premium

**VIX term-structure strategies.** Canonical rule: Simon & Campasano — short front VX when daily roll (contango) > 0.10pts, hedged with E-mini, 5-day hold — profitable 2007–2011 and "robust to transaction costs" in-sample, but [Quantpedia's out-of-sample tracking shows slightly negative alpha since publication](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/exploiting-term-structure-of-vix-futures). [Cheng (RFS, "The VIX Premium")](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/advance-article/doi/10.1093/rfs/hhy062/5017289) confirms a large ex-ante premium, but it's highly correlated with equities — paid for the same crash. VX bid-ask averages ~0.43% and exceeds 1.5% in crises — exactly when you must trade.

**The XIV lesson, quantified.** Feb 5, 2018: VIX 17→37 intraday; XIV −96% in one session ($1.9B → $63M), SVXY −93%, ~$3B of inverse-vol AUM destroyed in ~50 minutes, the products' own rebalancing feeding the spike ([CFA Institute FAJ "Volmageddon"](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products); [SixFigureInvesting post-mortem](https://www.sixfigureinvesting.com/2019/02/what-caused-the-february-5th-2018-volatility-spike-xiv-termination/)). Pre-2018 short-vol backtests showed Sharpe ~1.5–2 for years; one day repriced it. Any short-vol sleeve needs hard sizing caps (<~20% of book), defined-risk structure, and the assumption that a 2018 recurs.

**Defined-risk option selling.** Cboe's benchmarks are the honest net-ish record: the mechanical monthly SPX iron condor index [CNDR](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-volatility-management-with-cboes-bfly-and-cndr-indices/) was **≈ flat 2010–2024 (~−0.7%/yr)** after 9%/yr pre-2010; [PUT (put-write)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBOE_S%26P_500_PutWrite_Index) ~7.2%/yr over the 10 years to mid-2025 vs ~13% for S&P TR ([WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/us/insights/blog/why-fear-pays-the-case-for-put-writing-over-call-writing)) — lower vol/drawdown, but equity beta + short-vol beta, not alpha. Retail extra costs subtract further.

**Data:** VX daily settlements and term structure free from Cboe ([vixstructure.com](https://vixstructure.com/)); historical options chains are the hard part (CBOE DataShop/ORATS cost money; IBKR gives live chains but poor history). VX futures and SPX/XSP options tradeable at IBKR.

**Verdict:** premium real but it is **crash-risk beta**; mechanically harvested versions ≈ zero net since 2010; tail-rule versions decayed OOS. Lowest priority.

## 3. Calendar/seasonality

- **Turn-of-month: dead in the US.** [Quantseeker's 2025 replication](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/turn-of-the-month-strategies-do-they) (SPY/QQQ/IWM, sectors, intl ETFs): classical [0:3] window differences statistically zero for US equities vs the historical 12–20bp/day; a wider [−3:+3] window retains 5–12bp significance mostly in international ETFs. State-dependence support exists ([JIFMIM 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1042443126000259)), but as a standalone US strategy: arbitraged away.
- **Pre-FOMC drift: genuinely contested — and testable by us.** [Kurov, Gilbert & Wolfe](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3134546): the classic Lucca–Moench 24h drift disappeared after 2015. But [Quantseeker (data through Dec 2024)](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/trading-the-fed-the-pre-fomc-drift) finds a *shifted* window (close before meeting → close of meeting day) still strongly significant: ~4% CAGR on SPY at Sharpe 0.5–0.6 while in the market ~5% of days; a 2024 [Applied Economics study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00036846.2024.2322573) also finds drift in recent data. Eight trades/year, one liquid ETF, EOD fills — the single cheapest thing on this list to validate in our own harness.
- **Overnight/weekend.** Gross effect still enormous (Q3'20–Q3'25: SPY close→open +47% cumulative vs open→close +30% — [Cacciatore](https://medium.com/@ejcacciatore/a-dissection-of-market-returns-the-overnight-anomaly-and-the-call-for-a-24-5-rhythm-995309847fea)), but [Elm Wealth](https://elmwealth.com/night-moves-overnight-drift/) shows rolling 5-year edges fading since the 2008–2015 publications, and two trades/day at even 2bp round-trip ≈ −5%/yr — fatal. Use only to bias *when* other strategies execute (prefer holding overnight, entering near close). Related: [Bowles et al. "Anomaly Time" (JF 2024)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13372) — anomaly returns concentrate in days right after signal release; stale signals are dead signals.
- **Pre-holiday:** small positive abnormal returns persist for some US holidays in 2014–2018 data ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/pre-holiday-effect); mood-seasonality framework [Hirshleifer et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24676/revisions/w24676.rev1.pdf)), but bp-scale and unstable. Filter, not strategy.

## 4. Cross-asset carry

**FX carry.** Post-2022 rate divergence revived G10 carry, but differentials have renarrowed (~42bp compression since Jan 2024) and carry-pair vol rose ~35% y/y ([FXStreet/ING](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/fx-daily-g10-fx-carry-is-back-in-the-drivers-seat-202607100526)); the August-2024 yen-carry unwind was a live demonstration of the crash skew documented in [Daniel, Hodrick & Lu](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20433/w20433.pdf). Out-of-sample academic evidence is sobering ([JIMF 2024, "The out-of-sample performance of carry trades"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0261560624000299)); typical literature cost assumption ~50% of quoted bid-ask. Retail-feasible via IBKR spot FX or CME micro FX futures; expect Sharpe ~0.3–0.5 with severe negative skew. Risk premium, not alpha.

**Futures carry (Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt, JFE 2018, ["Carry"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X17302908)).** Diversified cross-asset carry Sharpe ~0.8 gross in-sample, but the result rests on breadth (dozens of markets); diversification/crisis properties flagged as unlikely to persist at full strength ([Return Stacked academic review](https://www.returnstacked.com/academic-review/carry/)). At €20k with micros maybe 5–8 markets can be held — the strategy's engine *is* breadth. **Verdict: real premium, structurally infeasible at this capital; an FX+bond-carry tilt inside T1's futures book is the most to take.**

## 5. ETF tactical rotation

**Dual momentum OOS.** Antonacci's GEM (published 2013/14) [extended backtests look fine](https://www.optimalmomentum.com/extended-backtest-of-global-equities-momentum/), but independent OOS accounting is negative: since publication GEM lagged a plain 60/40 and its drawdown edge "was mostly a 2008 story" ([Quant for Free replication](https://quant4free.com/analysis/dual-momentum/)); ReSolve's ["Craftsman" study](https://investresolve.com/global-equity-momentum-executive-summary/) showed results are hostage to lookback and rebalance-date luck (tracking error between equivalent specifications is huge).

**The Allocate Smartly evidence base.** They track 70+ published TAA strategies net of costs in near-real-time — the closest thing to a live-vs-backtest registry. Two honest findings: (a) essentially all TAA backtests are overfit to some degree, and they push authors (e.g., Keller's BAA→HAA) to simplify ([HAA writeup](https://allocatesmartly.com/hybrid-asset-allocation/), [FAQ](https://allocatesmartly.com/faqs/)); (b) **TAA as a class protected notably less in the 2022 bear than in prior downturns** ([2022 review](https://allocatesmartly.com/tactical-asset-allocation-performance-during-the-2022-bear-market/)) — 2022 was a slow grind where bonds fell too, breaking the "flee to Treasuries" mechanic most rely on.

**Verdict:** beta-timing/risk-management, not alpha. If run, assume live ≈ published backtest minus 2–4%/yr CAGR; monthly turnover is cheap at IBKR. Reasonable *portfolio wrapper* for personal capital; not a strategy-research payoff.

## 6. Event-driven at daily horizon

- **PEAD:** multi-day drift for non-microcap US stocks essentially gone since ~2006 — prices jump at announcement (Martineau 2022; [Quantpedia status](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/post-earnings-announcement-effect)). Survives in **microcaps / no-analyst names**, precisely where arbitrage costs are highest; ML/text-based surprise definitions revive it in-sample 2008–2019 ([FRL 2025 ML revival](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612325020057)) — treat as unvalidated. Retail verdict: only as a microcap niche; needs an earnings-surprise feed and wide-spread cost modeling.
- **Index inclusion: dead.** [Greenwood & Sammon, "The Disappearing Index Effect"](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf): S&P 500 addition pop fell 9.6% (late-90s) → 1.6% (early 2010s); deletion effect −16% (90s) → statistically zero 2010–2020. Don't build this.
- **Insider/buybacks: the live one.** Insider *purchases* (especially opportunistic, non-routine, small-cap) still predict returns in recent samples ([J. Empirical Finance 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092753982500088X)); [FRL 2024, "Insider filings as trading signals — does it pay to be fast?"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612324015435): the edge concentrates in the days right after Form 4 filing — a same/next-day EDGAR-scrape reaction at 2-day–2-month horizons is exactly our band, and EDGAR is free. Cost caveat: signals cluster in small names; model realistic spreads.

## 7. New / credible 2023–2026, retail-implementable

- **Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, ["Beat the Market" (SSRN 2024)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4824172):** SPY intraday momentum, claimed 19.6%/yr net, Sharpe 1.33. Flagged because it's everywhere — but it's *intraday* (falsified in our own harness for equities), practitioner reviews call results ["a bit too good to be true"](https://quantmacro.substack.com/p/paper-review-an-effective-intraday) (fill assumptions, stop-gap risk), and a [follow-up (Maróy, SSRN 2025)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5095349) claiming Sharpe >3 via parameter optimization is a textbook overfitting red flag. Skip as-published; see Appendix A §3 for the honest ES replication.
- **FOMC-window strategy (§3)** is arguably the best *new-ish* claim: recently re-validated through Dec 2024, trivial infrastructure, directly falsifiable in our harness within a day.
- **ETF short-term reversal with trend filter:** 2025/26 practitioner/Quantpedia-lineage work on 2006–2025 data (six liquid ETFs, 200-day MA filter + multi-day pullback trigger; e.g., [Vojtko et al. SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6979441)) — 2–10 day holds; treat as hypothesis generator, not evidence.
- **ETF/leveraged-fund rebalancing flow anticipation** ([Wang, Yao & Yelekenova, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4324054)) — real mechanism, but tradeable residue at daily bars post-Greenwood-Sammon is doubtful.
- **Ongoing free replication sources** for decay detection: [Quantseeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/) (weekly paper replications), Quantpedia OOS strategy tracking, Allocate Smartly's live TAA registry.

## Bottom line for this project

1. **T1 (TSMOM)** remains the best-supported thread, with three concrete refinements: vol-target as a first-class component (benchmark against vol-targeted long-only), blend medium+slow speeds only (fast trend microstructurally dead), don't count on crisis alpha for fast shocks. Honest net expectation: Sharpe 0.3–0.5.
2. **Highest-value cheap experiments:** FOMC window (8 trades/yr, EOD, contested in literature → our harness settles it) and insider-purchase reaction (free EDGAR data, edge concentrated at our exact horizon).
3. **Run as beta, if at all:** defensive ETF rotation (risk management for personal capital, minus 2–4%/yr vs backtest), small capped put-write sleeve.
4. **Dead / skip:** US turn-of-month, index inclusion, large-cap PEAD, overnight-effect trading, unhedged short-vol, mechanical iron condors, full cross-asset carry at retail scale.
